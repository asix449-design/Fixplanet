Year **400**. Rome is more than a thousand years from its founding. On the map the empire still runs from Britain to Egypt. In life it is already two countries. In the West Emperor Honorius, in the East — Arcadius. Both sons of Theodosius. The father died in 395 and gave the realm to two young heirs.

Christianity since 380 is the official religion. Pagan temples are closed by decree. Some old families still honor the former gods at home. In the squares this is no longer the state's main faith.

Goths live not beyond the border but inside the empire: in the Balkans and in the Roman army. Ten years until Alaric takes Rome.

### Western Rome
The real capital is no longer Rome. The court sits in Milan; soon it will move to Ravenna — safer because of the marshes. Honorius on the throne is weak. The country is run by his guardian **Stilicho**, a general of Vandal stock. He is the last major man who still holds the West.

The Rhine is still counted a Roman border. In the winter of 406 Vandals, Alans, and Suebi will cross it on the ice. After that Gaul will start to be torn to pieces. In 400 that is still ahead.

Britain is formally in the empire, but soldiers are more and more often taken to the mainland. In a few years the island will be left almost without legions.

The city of Rome itself is rich in churches and old palaces. Senators remember greatness. They do not raise an army. There is less bread than a hundred years ago. The main fear is simple and close: the Goths.

**Alaric**, king of the Visigoths, served Rome and did not get what he was promised: land, grain, a worthy place. The Eastern empire tries to shove him onto the West. The West does not know how to pay. In 410 he will take Rome. In 400 he is already not a rumor from the steppe, but a political problem.

Africa is still calm and feeds Italy with grain. Spain and Gaul are still Roman on the map.

### Eastern Rome
Constantinople is a living capital: walls, harbor, court, officials. Arcadius too is a weak ruler, but the state under him is stronger than the western one. There is money, there is Egypt with grain, there is Syria and Asia Minor.

On the eastern border the Persians are dangerous, yet they do not stand at the gates every year.

Here they more often speak Greek than Latin. Bishops argue how to teach correctly about Christ. From these disputes come exile, street fights, and future church schisms. John Chrysostom in the capital quarrels with the imperial court. A bishop already weighs almost like a governor.

### Persia
The **Sasanians** rule. The court rests on Zoroastrianism and heavy cavalry. The state is dense, not loose. With the Roman East they now trade, now fight. Neither side collapses in 400.

Around this time Shah Yazdegerd I even treats Christians relatively mildly at home. For Persia that is more exception than rule.

### China
The north of the country is a patchwork of kingdoms. Nomadic and semi-nomadic rulers wear Chinese titles and take tax from Chinese villages. The north's great attempt to conquer the south already failed in 383 at the Fei River.

The south — Eastern Jin dynasty, capital at Jiankang, future Nanjing. Noble families fled there from the north. They write poems, argue about old books, and generals in fact spin the country. Soon one southern dynasty will replace another. The meaning is the same: weak court, its own army.

The Silk Road works when there is no war between the cities.

### India and Africa
In India the **Gupta** power is rising. Exactly the time of Chandragupta II: gold coins, Sanskrit, temples, trade. While Rome in the West loses order, India enters its calm and rich stretch.

In East Africa Aksum is strong. It is already Christian and holds the Red Sea: ivory, gold, trade with India and the Mediterranean.

### North and steppe
The Huns already press from the east. Attila himself is not yet here — he will appear in half a century. But because of that pressure the Goths shifted toward Rome, and Rome does not know where to put them. The "Great Migration of Peoples" here is not a textbook name, but a queue of tribes at the border.

There are no Slavic principalities on the Dnieper. The forests of eastern Europe live by their own ways, without emperors and without chronicles.

In Britain stand Roman cities and Celtic communities. In the north of the island — Picts. Saxons so far come as raiders, not as new masters.

### Faith
In the villages the old gods are still remembered. The state bets on the church. The Olympic games were banned in the 390s. In Alexandria the pagan sanctuary has already been smashed.

Christianity is not one. The court holds to Nicene teaching. Many Goths are baptized otherwise, in the Arian pattern: for them Christ is not equal to the Father. So a Goth can be a Christian — and still remain alien to a Roman bishop.

In Armenia and Aksum Christianity is already the state religion. In Persia Christians are a minority under watch.

### What it feels like
Year 400 — a year when the official map is still handsome, and life under it is already different.

On paper one empire with two emperors. In fact the West holds on Stilicho and on the habit of provinces paying tax. The East is learning to be a separate state and does not say it aloud.

A senator in Rome looks at old columns and speaks of the eternal City. A Gothic king counts how much grain and rank he is owed. A Chinese man of letters on the Yangtze writes of lost northern capitals. An Indian court mints gold and does not look toward the Rhine.

What comes right after:
- 402 — the western court finally settles in Ravenna;
- 406 — the breakthrough across the Rhine;
- 408 — Stilicho killed by his own;
- 410 — Alaric enters Rome;
- 439 — Vandals take Carthage; Italy loses African grain;
- 451 — battle of the Catalaunian Fields with Attila;
- 476 — in the West there will be no emperor of its own.

Year 400 — the last round year when in the West you can still say "we are Rome" and almost believe it. Constantinople's walls will stand. Rome's — will not.
