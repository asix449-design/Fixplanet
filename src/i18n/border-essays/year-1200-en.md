Year **1200**. The pope in Rome is the strongest in a century: **Innocent III**. He is already thinking of a new crusade. In four years these crusaders, instead of Jerusalem, will take Christian Constantinople. At the other end of the steppe a man named Temüjin is not yet known to everyone as Genghis Khan. The kurultai that will make him “khan of khans” will be in 1206. In 1200 he has already gathered the Mongols into a fist and is looking at the neighbours.

Europe is dense, Gothic, confident. To the Mongols in Rus — twenty-three years. To Magna Carta — fifteen.

### Europe
France. King **Philip II Augustus** — a rare case: a king who actually gathers a kingdom. He squeezes Normandy and other pieces from the English (that will happen in the coming years; the conflict is already on). Paris grows. The university is no longer a circle but a force. They speak Latin, argue about Aristotle, who returned to Europe through Arabic translations.

England. On the throne since 1199 **John Lackland**, brother of Richard the Lionheart. Richard died of a wound a year ago. John will soon quarrel with everyone: barons, pope, France. In 1215 they will put Magna Carta under his nose. In 1200 he is simply a new king with a bad temper and a leaky treasury.

Germany. An empire without a firm centre; the Hohenstaufen still hold on. On the Baltic Germans and Danes baptise and conquer pagan lands. The Sword Brothers in Livonia are already at work.

Italy. Cities grow rich on eastern trade. Venice counts ships and the crusaders’ debts — that is what will ruin Constantinople for her, or rather make her an accomplice in the sack.

Pope Innocent III meddles everywhere: coronation, marriage, heresy, crusade. He will not spare the Albigensians in southern France either — the great slaughter of heretics begins in 1209. In 1200 the church is at its peak. It seems Rome again commands kings. It seems.

In the north the Swiss Confederation is still early (1291). Norway, Denmark, Sweden live their own feuds. The Hansa is still forming.

### Byzantium and the crusading wind-up
In Constantinople the Angelos dynasty. The empire is weak after Manzikert long ago and after its own coups. Bulgaria under Kaloyan again beats the Greeks. Cumans raid. Emperor Alexios III hangs on intrigue.

The pope writes of church union and of a campaign. The Fourth Crusade has been preached since 1198. Officially the goal — Egypt, then Jerusalem. In fact there will not be enough money to pay Venice for the ships. Debt, the cunning doge Dandolo, a runaway Byzantine prince — and in 1204 the Latins will take and sack the second Rome. In 1200 you can still not know that. You can preach a holy cause and not see where it will turn.

The Holy Land. Jerusalem since 1187 with Saladin and his heirs. The crusaders keep a narrow coastal strip: Acre, Tyre. Richard’s Third Crusade almost returned the city — did not. The truce is rotting.

### The steppe: the future Genghis Khan
In Mongolia Temüjin has already beaten the main enemies among his own: Merkits, Tayichiuds, part of the Keraites. He is not yet master of the world. He is a leader who knows how to divide loot not only by clan but by loyalty. That is a new mechanic of the steppe.

China to his north — the Jin empire, the Jurchens who once humiliated the Mongols. To the west — the Tanguts of Xi Xia. South of Jin — the Chinese Song empire, rich and vulnerable. In 1200 the map is still the old one. In twenty years it will be unrecognisable.

### Rus
Kiev has long not been the only centre. Strongest of all in the northeast is the **Vladimir-Suzdal land**. In Vladimir, Vsevolod the Big Nest — the most powerful prince of Rus at this hour. Novgorod trades, the veche noise, invites and expels princes. Galicia-Volhynia looks to Hungary and Poland. Chernigov, Smolensk, Polotsk — their own thrones, their own quarrels.

Cumans now father-in-law, now enemy. To the Kalka of 1223, where Russians and Cumans together will fall under the reconnaissance of Jebe and Subutai — still a generation. In 1200 the princes cut each other as usual. Batu’s invasion does not haunt their dreams.

Orthodoxy their own, after the schism of 1054 with Rome. The pope is far. The steppe is close.

### Asia further
**China** is cut in two. North — Jin. South — Song: canals, cities, paper, gunpowder, river fleets. The most advanced economy in the world. It does not know that the Mongols are learning to take cities.

**Japan.** Just now, in 1192, Minamoto no Yoritomo became shogun. The Kamakura shogunate: there is an emperor, a military house rules. Samurai are no longer just servants but a stratum.

**Near East.** After Saladin the Ayyubids divide Syria and Egypt. The Baghdad caliph still sits, but that is more shrine than power. To Hulagu and the burning of Baghdad — 58 years.

**India.** In the north the Muslim Delhi Sultanate is only getting on its feet after the Ghurid campaigns. The south — its own Hindu kingdoms.

**Cambodia.** Angkor still lives; the Khmer empire is huge, but no longer eternal.

### Africa and the Americas
Egypt — the centre of the Islamic world by money and grain. The Maghreb has its own dynasties. In the Sahel proto-states form, from which Mali will later come.

In Mexico the Toltecs are already past, the Aztecs not yet an empire. In the Andes their own cultures. Vikings in Vinland were two hundred years ago and left. For Europe the western ocean is an edge, not a road.

### What it feels like
Year 1200 — a high moment of the European Middle Ages: cathedrals up, the pope loud, the knight still main in the picture. And at the same time — silence before two hurricanes: crusaders will strike their own Greeks, the steppe will strike everyone.

A merchant in Venice counts interest on the fleet. A prince in Vladimir counts sons. A monk in France preaches the Holy Sepulchre. Temüjin counts horses. None of them reads one newspaper. In thirty years the map from Kiev to Beijing will be common — in blood.

What follows:
- 1202–1204 — the Fourth Crusade and the sack of Constantinople;
- 1206 — Genghis Khan;
- 1209 — the Albigensian Crusade;
- 1215 — Magna Carta and the Mongol capture of Jurchen Beijing;
- 1223 — Kalka;
- 1237–1240 — Batu in Rus;
- 1258 — Baghdad.

1200 is the last quiet year when you could pretend the world consisted of familiar neighbours. The steppe has already gathered. The pope has already sent preachers. The ships are already ordered.
