Year **1600**. Japan ends a century and a half of civil wars with one battle. England issues merchants a paper for trade with India — from this an empire will grow. In Rome on a square they burn a monk for ideas about an infinite cosmos. In Moscow Boris Godunov rules: he is not of the old Rurikid dynasty, and the country does not yet know that in a year a terrible famine will begin, and then the Time of Troubles.

### Europe
- **17 February, Rome, Campo de' Fiori.** Giordano Bruno is burned — a monk, a philosopher. The Inquisition tried him for heresy: he said the Universe is infinite and there are many worlds, and he refused to recant. The pyre is public. Nine years later, in 1609, Galileo will assemble a spyglass, point it at the sky and see mountains on the Moon and the moons of Jupiter. That is, the ideas for which Bruno was executed will soon begin to be tested with instruments. Galileo himself will fall under church trial much later, in 1633.
- **31 December.** Queen Elizabeth I of England signs the charter of the **English East India Company**. This is not a state, but a society of merchants: the queen gives them a monopoly to trade "east of the Cape of Good Hope" — that is, with India and beyond. At first spices and cloth. Later this company will hold whole regions of India and collect taxes there. The Dutch will create their East India Company in 1602 and at once begin to push the Portuguese off the spice islands.
- France: King Henry IV has already reconciled Catholics and Protestants with the Edict of Nantes (1598). In 1600 he fights Savoy over the town of Saluzzo. At the same time the French try to dig in in Canada: the king grants a captain the right to the fur trade; the Tadoussac factory appears.
- Sweden: at Linköping in Holy Week supporters of King Sigismund (also king of Poland) are executed. Power is taken by his uncle Charles — soon he will become Charles IX.
- Near Prague the astronomers Tycho Brahe and Johannes Kepler meet. One has the best star observations in Europe, the other a new mathematics of orbits. From this meeting the laws of planetary motion will later come.
- Spain still seems Europe's first power: silver from America, huge colonies, a "golden age" of painting and literature. But the treasury is already leaky. Portugal since 1580 is under one crown with Spain — they call it the Iberian Union.

### Russia
The tsar is **Boris Godunov**. The last legitimate Rurikid, Fyodor Ivanovich, died in 1598 without children. The Zemsky Sobor chose Boris: he was Fyodor's brother-in-law and for many years in fact ruled under him. For many boyars he is "not the right" tsar — not of the old dynasty.

Godunov strengthens the southern border (the fortress Tsaryov-Borisov is built), pushes Russians into Siberia, thinks of bringing scholars from the German lands to Moscow and even something like a university. It will go badly: distrust gets in the way, and the fact that the country is about to fall into trouble.

Among the noble families he fears are the **Romanovs**. They are kin of Ivan the Terrible's first wife. In 1600–1601 a case of "witchcraft" against the tsar is brought against them. The head of the house, Fyodor Nikitich Romanov, is forcibly tonsured as a monk and given the name **Filaret**. That is how they removed a dangerous man without execution: he is alive, but as a boyar and a possible claimant to influence — destroyed. His wife too is sent to a convent.

The irony of history: in a few years the Time of Troubles will begin, the Godunovs will perish, and this very Filaret will become patriarch, and his son Mikhail in 1613 will become the first tsar of the Romanov dynasty. In 1600 Godunov was simply clearing the field.

Already close is something else: from 1601 three years in a row there will be crop failure and famine. Hundreds of thousands died. After that the pretender False Dmitry will appear, and the state will for a time almost fall apart. In 1600 itself this is not yet visible — only a taut silence.

### Asia
**Japan. 21 October — the Battle of Sekigahara.**
For a century and a half the princes cut each other (the Sengoku age). The country was almost gathered by Oda Nobunaga and Toyotomi Hideyoshi, but Hideyoshi died in 1598, leaving a young son. The regents fell out.

In the narrow valley of Sekigahara two coalitions of vassals meet: the "eastern" headed by **Tokugawa Ieyasu** and the "western" headed by Ishida Mitsunari. Fog, mud, a few hours of fighting. Betrayal decides it: part of the western army (Kobayakawa) goes over to Tokugawa. The west is smashed; Ishida is later executed.

Ieyasu after that is master of Japan. He will take the title of shogun in 1603. The Edo age will begin: the capital in effect in Edo (future Tokyo), peace inside the country for almost 250 years, tight control of the princes. For Christians and Portuguese traders this is not good news — later Japan will almost close itself from the outside world.

By the way, Tokugawa in this war had European cannons. In spring 1600 the Dutch ship *Liefde* reached Japan. Of the whole crew about two dozen remained alive, among them the Englishman William Adams. His cannons came in handy. Adams would later stay in Japan forever.

**China.** The Ming dynasty still rules, but already weakens: embezzlement, heavy taxes; in the north the Manchus rise. They will set up the new Qing dynasty in 1644.

**India.** The Mughal Empire under Padishah Akbar — one of the richest powers in the world. Akbar will die in 1605. The court relatively tolerant in religion, the treasury huge.

**The Ottoman Empire** still huge, but Suleiman's best years are behind. The neighbour — Shia Safavid Iran, the eternal opponent.

### The Americas and the seas
- On 19 February in Peru the volcano Huaynaputina explodes — one of the strongest eruptions in the history of South America. Ash for years, crop failures. Some link this also to the famine in Russia of 1601–1603 (through cooling), though that is disputed.
- Brazil is already a Portuguese sugar colony: plantations, slaves from Africa. Spanish America lives on the silver of the Potosí mines. The empires of the Aztecs and Incas are long gone.
- The Dutch sail around the world. Off Manila van Noort sinks a Spanish galleon.

### What it feels like
Year 1600 — already not the Middle Ages, not yet "our" seventeenth century with the scientific revolution and absolutism in full form.

A merchant company gets from the queen the right to do business like a little state. Japan in one day chooses who will rule for two and a half centuries. The church executes a man for the cosmos — and in the same Europe they are already assembling lenses. Moscow tries to look like a solid tsardom and at the same time cuts its own boyars down to the root.

### What follows
- 1601–1603 — famine in Russia;
- 1603 — Ieyasu officially shogun; in England Elizabeth will die, the crown goes to James — personal union of England and Scotland;
- 1605 — Godunov's death, the start of the full Time of Troubles;
- 1618 — the Thirty Years' War in Europe.

1600 — the year of a charter for the ocean, a pyre on a Roman square and six hours in a Japanese gorge. Who won the field — set the rules of the land. Who issued the paper for spices — set the rules of the sea.
