Year **300**. Rome is almost 1,050 years from its founding. The empire is still huge — from Britain to Egypt — but one man no longer rules it. For almost ten years the **tetrarchy** scheme has stood: four rulers for one realm. In Persia sit the Sasanians, fiercer than the Parthians. In China the Jin dynasty has only just seemingly united the country after the Three Kingdoms — and at once begins to gnaw itself from within.

Christians are already many. In three years Diocletian will try to burn them out. It will not work.

### Rome
The main man is **Diocletian**. Not a senatorial legend, but a soldier from the Balkans. He grasped a simple thing: the 3rd-century empire broke because one emperor could not make every war. The solution — cut power apart.

By 300 the tetrarchy already works:
- two "Augusti" — the seniors;
- two "Caesars" — the juniors, heirs and deputies.

Diocletian holds the East; residence more often Nicomedia (in Asia Minor, not Rome). The West — Maximian. The Caesars — Galerius and Constantius Chlorus, father of the future Constantine. The city of Rome is still sacred, but the capital in practice is wherever the staff and army are. The Senate is almost decoration.

Borders are held again: Rhine, Danube, East against the Persians. Inside — new bureaucracy, new taxes, an attempt to freeze prices by edict (works poorly: the market is smarter than a decree). The army is larger. Provinces smaller, so a governor cannot secede with his piece.

For a townsman in Antioch or Carthage life is still Roman: bath, court, coin with a profile. For a peasant — tax heavier than under the Antonines. The 3rd-century crisis is formally behind. The fear remains.

Christianity. No longer a handful in the catacombs. There are churches, bishops, their own disputes about the faith. Diocletian long looked the other way. In 303 the "Great Persecution" begins: tear down temples, burn books, purge Christian officials. This is the empire's last big attempt to stay pagan the old way. In ten years Constantine will see another calculation.

Nearby, a little after 300, in Armenia King Tiridates and Gregory the Illuminator will make Christianity the state religion — earlier than Rome itself.

### Persia
The **Sasanian** dynasty. They call themselves heirs of the ancient Achaemenids; Zoroastrianism is the court's prop. For Rome this is a more serious enemy than Parthia: more regular, more ideological, hits harder.

The Mesopotamian border is forever hot. Captive Romans build Persian cities. Captive Persians — the reverse. A draw with pauses for slaughter.

### China
The Three Kingdoms ended in 280: Jin united the map. By 300 unity is already rotting. The emperor is weak, the princes strong. Soon the "War of the Eight Princes" — civil war of family against family. Nomadic peoples press the northern borders. Some of them are already inside the empire: settled as federates, serve and wait their hour.

In a few years northern China will start being carved up by "barbarian" kingdoms. Jin will flee south, beyond the Yangtze. North–South split for a long time. In 300 you can still not see this if you sit in Luoyang and trust the decrees.

Paper, official exams, scholarly disputes — culture has not died. The state is ill.

### India and beyond
Northern India still without the Gupta Empire (they come a little later). The Kushans are no longer what they were. Trade with the West lives, but Rome buys less: after the 3rd century there is not as much money or people.

Aksum in East Africa is rising: Red Sea, ivory, gold, soon its own Christianity.

Japan — Yamato burial mounds, royal power taking shape. Korea — three kingdoms; Chinese writing and Buddhism on the way.

### North and steppe
Beyond the Rhine Germans denser: Franks, Alemanni, Goths by the Black Sea. Rome sometimes beats them, sometimes hires them. Goths not yet in Italy. Until Adrianople (378) and the sack of Rome (410) — a lifetime.

On the lands of future Rus' — forest, hillforts, no Kiev. Slavs as a great force are not yet on Roman pages.

The steppe between China and the Black Sea lives by its own alliances. Xiongnu/Huns already history; other names on the stage. The Great Migration in the full European sense is not yet here, but people are already shifting.

### America
Teotihuacan in Mexico — a huge city of pyramids and streets. Maya in Classic stride. Incas still far off. For Rome this continent does not exist.

### What it feels like
Year 300 — repair after a fire.

Diocletian thinks he has invented a perpetual-motion machine of power: four rule, two resign, two rise. In five years he will indeed leave for his garden in Split — a rare emperor who left of his own will. The system without him will quickly go off the rails. Constantine, son of Caesar Constantius, in 300 is still an officer in the East, not master of the world.

A Christian in Nicomedia can still serve in the army and hope it will blow over. In three years — a search. A pagan priest thinks Rome's gods and the emperor's edict are one bond. Both are wrong.

A Chinese prince thinks that after unification you can divide the spoils. Soon the capital itself will be the spoils.

The world of 300 is still Mediterranean–Chinese: two big machines for counting people and grain. Between them Persia and the trading seas. The "barbarian" north is larger than Rome is ready to admit.

What follows:
- 303 — Diocletian's persecution;
- 305 — his resignation;
- 306 — Constantine proclaimed at York;
- 312 — Milvian Bridge, "in this sign conquer";
- 313 — Edict of Milan, Christianity permitted;
- 324–330 — a new Rome on the Bosporus;
- 376–378 — Goths cross the Danube, Adrianople;
- 316–420s — Jin loses northern China.

Year 300 — the last year when you can seriously imagine a pagan empire as the forever norm. The norm is already different: many rulers, many gods, and one of the cults is about to stop being forbidden. Diocletian did not plan this. History rarely asks the planner.
