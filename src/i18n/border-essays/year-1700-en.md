Year **1700**. Europe switches to a new calendar. In the north three countries decide that the young Swedish king is easy prey. In the west the childless king of Spain dies — and the whole continent prepares to divide his empire. In Russia Peter has just made peace with the Turks and at once pushes into the Baltic. The year begins as a calculation. It ends with the Russians smashed at Narva.

### Europe: two wars at once
**The Great Northern War begins**

Sweden in 1700 is the great power of the Baltic. It holds Finland, Estonia, Livonia (roughly today's Estonia and Latvia), Ingria (the land around future St Petersburg) and chunks of northern Germany. King **Charles XII** is only 18. The neighbours think: a boy — now we take the shore.

Against Sweden a coalition forms:
- Denmark–Norway,
- Saxony and the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth (they share one ruler — Augustus II the Strong),
- Russia of Peter I.

In February the Saxons without a declaration of war push toward Riga. The Danes strike Sweden's ally in Holstein.

Charles does not lose his head. In summer he suddenly lands near Copenhagen. Denmark quickly asks for peace (Travendal, August) and exits the war. The English and Dutch even help with a fleet: they need a quiet Baltic, because in the west another war is about to flare.

Peter learns that peace with the Ottoman Empire has finally been concluded for 30 years, and on **30 August 1700** declares war on Sweden. The pretext simple for the people: the Swedes once insulted the tsar in Riga. The real goal — to win back access to the Baltic Sea, lost already in the Time of Troubles.

The Russian army — large, poorly trained, with foreign commanders. It besieges the Swedish fortress of **Narva**. Charles drops Denmark, drives the army into Estonia. On **30 November 1700** (new style) about 10 thousand Swedes beat 30–40 thousand Russians. Storm, snow, panic, the cannons surrender. Peter by then has already left the camp — he left the army to Field Marshal de Croÿ. Total shame.

Charles decides Russia is no longer dangerous and goes off to beat Augustus in Poland. That is a nine-year mistake. In 1709 there will be Poltava. In 1721 Sweden will lose great-power status; Russia will get the Baltic and become an empire. But in 1700 itself the only winner is the eighteen-year-old Swede.

**The Spanish Succession — a time bomb**

On 1 November Charles II of Spain dies. The last Spanish Habsburg, childless, ill. In the will the throne goes to Philip of Anjou, grandson of French king Louis XIV. Louis accepts the crown for his grandson: he becomes Philip V.

Spain then is not only Spain. It is southern Italy, the Spanish Netherlands, huge America. If France and Spain end up under one family roof, the rest of Europe will choke. England, Holland, Austria will not stand it. The War of the Spanish Succession will begin in 1701 and last until 1714.

In November the Elector of Brandenburg agrees with the emperor: for help in the coming war he will be raised to king of Prussia. The coronation — in 1701. So is born the kingdom from which Bismarck's Germany will later come.

**The calendar**

From 1 January Protestant Western Europe (except England) switches to the Gregorian calendar — the one we use now. The Julian was already behind by 10–11 days. Russia that same day begins to count years "from the Nativity of Christ," not "from the creation of the world," as in Byzantium. Peter will introduce the Gregorian calendar itself only in 1918 — not he, but the Bolsheviks; in 1700 they simply change the year-count point and move New Year to 1 January.

### Russia
Peter I has ruled for a long time already, but these very years make him a "European" tsar. The Great Embassy has just ended: he himself went to learn shipbuilding in Holland and England. After the 1698 revolt he crushed the streltsy cruelly. He cuts beards, builds a fleet.

Narva 1700 is humiliation. Peter does not break: he starts forging the army anew, casting cannons, building a fleet. In three years he will found St Petersburg — on land that still has to be won.

### Asia
**China.** The Qing dynasty, Emperor Kangxi. The country after the Manchu conquest is already relatively calm, huge, strong. For Beijing Europe is distant exotica, not an equal partner.

**India.** The Mughal Empire after Akbar and Aurangzeb begins to crack: too big, too many wars. The European companies (English, Dutch, French) still sit in the ports and trade, do not rule.

**The Ottoman Empire** recently lost a big war with the Austrians (Treaty of Karlowitz 1699). They will not take Vienna again. Still an empire, but no longer the one of Suleiman's day.

**Japan** is closed. The Tokugawa shogunate. Foreigners almost forbidden, Christianity banned. Two and a half centuries of relative internal peace continue.

### The Americas and the ocean
On 26 January off the Pacific shore of North America — the monstrous Cascadia earthquake (about magnitude 9). A tsunami crosses the ocean to Japan. Europeans on the spot barely record it: there are still few colonies there.

English, French, Spanish colonies in America already stand. Sugar from the Caribbean, furs of Canada, silver of Mexico and Peru. Slave labour from Africa — an ordinary part of this machine. In Pennsylvania William Penn begins to speak of freeing Black people — that is still a rarity and a circle, not imperial policy.

Brazil gives Portugal more and more gold (Minas Gerais is just ramping up in these years).

World population — on the order of 600–700 million. More than half — in Asia.

### What it feels like
Year 1700 — the year when the old empires still look eternal, and the new ones are already coming through the door.

Sweden seems invincible — and in twenty years it will be shoved off the Baltic. Spain seems worldwide — and its inheritance will be torn apart by war. Russia seems backward — and after Narva Peter will make a life programme out of defeat.

Louis XIV is still the "Sun King," but France will soon be worn down by the war for the Spanish crown. Newton has already written the *Principia*, science exists, but kings still decide everything with cannons.

### What follows
- 1701 — the War of the Spanish Succession and the Kingdom of Prussia;
- 1703 — the founding of St Petersburg;
- 1707 — England and Scotland will become Great Britain;
- 1709 — Poltava;
- 1714 — end of the war for Spain, the Hanoverians on the British throne;
- 1721 — the Treaty of Nystad, Russia declares itself an empire.

1700 — the last year when one could pretend the Baltic was forever Swedish and Moscow forever far from Europe. After Narva Peter hated that pretence. Charles decided he had won. Both were wrong about the timing.
