Year **1500**. Europe still thinks it stands at the centre of the world. In fact the Portuguese have already rounded Africa; Columbus has already bumped into America and to his death was sure it was Asia. This year another fleet goes to India for pepper — and on the way "finds" Brazil. In Moscow Ivan III wrings land from Lithuania. In Italy the French and local dukes cut each other over cities. The Reformation is not yet here. Luther is still a monk, not a blast packet.

### Europe
This is already not quite the Middle Ages and not yet that "new age" later called the Early Modern period.

Italy is the main stage. French king Louis XII pushes into Lombardy. In April at Novara they seize the Milanese duke Ludovico Sforza, the same one who commissioned Leonardo. Milan is French again. Popes, Venice, the emperor — all in this mess with their own knives.

The Holy Roman Empire — a patchwork quilt of princes. At Augsburg the Reichstag tries to bring at least some order to courts and circles. It works on paper.

Spain after Isabella and Ferdinand has already expelled the Jews and the Muslims from the Emirate of Granada, holds a ton of American "not-yet-understood" shore and argues with Portugal whose ocean it is.

The church is huge, rich, used to selling indulgences and living like a court. Seventeen years until the Reformation hits it. For now everyone is still one Western church, if you do not count the long-since-split Orthodox East.

They print books. Artists in Italy already paint not only saints face-on. Leonardo, Michelangelo, Raphael — these very years. But for a peasant in the field year 1500 looks like year 1400, only the taxes are different.

### The sea: why Brazil at all
Portuguese king Manuel I already knows: Vasco da Gama in 1498 reached India around Africa. Spices are money you do not have to hand to Venetians and Arab middlemen.

On 9 March 1500 a second great fleet leaves Lisbon for India: 13 ships, about twelve hundred men, commander — **Pedro Álvares Cabral**, a nobleman, not a great sailor, but one of their own. With him experienced captains, including Bartolomeu Dias, the one who first rounded the south of Africa.

To catch the right wind they swing far west in the Atlantic. On 22 April they see a mountain. That is the shore of future Brazil. Cabral decides: the land is on the Portuguese side of the Tordesillas line (Spain and Portugal drew it in 1494: "west for you, east for us"). He plants a cross, writes the king, sends one ship back with the letter. They meet the locals, exchange gifts. Then the fleet goes on to Africa and India as planned.

The Spanish that same year are also poking about that shore: Pinzón already in January bumps into the north of Brazil. Who was "first" — historians will argue later. Politically the paper won: Brazil will be Portuguese, the rest of the big chunk of America — Spanish. No one yet knows how huge the continent is. Columbus is still alive and still confuses the islands with Asia.

In India Cabral will fare badly: at Calicut the factory is massacred, he answers with fire on ships, leaves for another prince, loads pepper and returns with an incomplete fleet. The scheme for years ahead: trade plus cannon.

### Russia
The sovereign is **Ivan III**, later called the Great. He has already gathered around Moscow Tver, Novgorod, a pile of principalities, stopped bowing to the Horde. A double-headed eagle on the seal, a brick Kremlin, a wife who once was the Byzantine princess Sophia Palaiologina — hence the habit of feeling like a "Third Rome," though those words come a little later.

In 1500 war with the Grand Duchy of Lithuania. The reason is simple: several princes from the borderlands defect to Ivan together with their towns. Lithuania calls that theft of vassals. Ivan does not wait and strikes first.

On 14 July on the Vedrosha River, not far from Dorogobuzh, the Russians smash the Lithuanian army. Hetman Ostrogski himself is taken prisoner. The war will last until 1503. Moscow will take a chunk of lands to the west — Chernihiv, Novhorod-Siverskyi and more. This is not "annexation of Ukraine" in the modern sense. It is the gathering of pieces of former Rus that for two hundred years sat under Vilnius.

In the south the ally is the Crimean khan. In the northwest Livonia will soon push in. The country is not yet Peter's empire and not "Russia" on every European map, but already not a set of appanages.

### Asia
**The Ottomans.** Sultan Bayezid II. The Turks finish off Venetian scraps in Greece, fight at sea. Constantinople has been Turkish for half a century. A generation yet until Suleiman the Magnificent.

**Iran.** In a year, in 1501, young Ismail of the Safavid house will take Tabriz and start a Shia empire. In 1500 that is still being prepared: nomads, an order, luck.

In Central Asia the Uzbek khan Muhammad Shaybani takes Bukhara and Samarkand. Timur's heirs weaken.

**India.** The Mughal Empire is not yet here (Babur will come in 1526). In the north the Delhi Sultanate is living out its days. On the Malabar coast — princes like the Zamorin of Calicut, through whom the pepper goes. The Portuguese are only wedging in.

**China.** The Ming dynasty. Emperor Hongzhi. The country is rich, huge; it has already cooled toward Zheng He's distant voyages. To Europe it is almost invisible; to itself it seems the Middle Kingdom.

**Japan** in fragmentation; a hundred years until Tokugawa unification.

### The Americas, still without the word "colony"
The Aztecs in Mexico at their peak: Tenochtitlan — a huge city on water. The Incas in the Andes hold an empire from the mountains to the shore. Neither knows that in twenty to thirty years people will come with horses, smallpox and a thirst for gold. Cortés will land in 1519. For now 1500 for them is their ordinary year of wars and tribute.

The Caribbean already burns with plague and slavery: Columbus and his heirs have broken the world on the islands. The mainland is still almost untouched.

### What it feels like
Year 1500 — the year when the world map cracks, and in most people's heads it is still round the old way: Jerusalem in the centre, the edge of the earth somewhere beyond Portugal.

The king in Lisbon counts pepper. The king in Castile counts islands. The prince in Moscow counts towns along the Desna. The pope counts indulgences. The artist in Florence counts the proportions of the body.

No one this year says "we live on the threshold of globalisation." They say: monsoon, heresy, patrimony, spice, a cross on a new shore.

### What follows
- 1501 — the Safavids in Iran;
- 1503 — peace of Moscow and Lithuania, end of this war;
- 1517 — Luther and the 95 theses;
- 1519–1521 — Cortés and the end of the Aztecs;
- 1522 — Magellan's ship will circle the earth.

1500 — the year Brazil entered the European notebook almost by accident, and Vedrosha proved that Moscow was already not Vilnius's younger brother. The ocean became a road. The land between Smolensk and Bryansk — too.
