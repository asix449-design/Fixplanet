Year **200** is no longer a calm antique noon, as in 100. The empires still stand, but something creaks inside each of them. Earth's population — roughly 190–260 million.

### Europe and Rome
- The emperor is **Septimius Severus**. An African from Lepcis Magna, a soldier who won the civil war after Commodus's murder. The Severan dynasty: power rests on the army; the Senate is already decoration.
- Parthian campaigns have just roared through. In 198 the Romans took Ctesiphon again. Severus made northern Mesopotamia a province. Hatra he never took — the fortress held.
- In 200 itself he travels Syria, Palestine, and Arabia. Looks at what is left after wars and plague. He takes Numidia from the proconsul of Africa and makes it an imperial province — control closer to the throne.
- After the Antonine Plague (from 165 and in waves after) there are fewer people, legions harder to fill, prices jump. The "good emperors" are over. Ahead — Severus's sons Caracalla and Geta, and that will be no fairy tale.
- Christians are already noticeable. Severus in Judea seems to forbid new conversions to Judaism and Christianity. The cult of Mithras among soldiers is no less popular.
- The Rhine and Danube frontier is tense. Germans press harder than a hundred years ago. Britain is still Roman; Hadrian's Wall stands.

Rome is still huge and rich. But this is already an empire tired of winning.

### Asia
**China — end of Han, start of chaos**  
- Formally Emperor Xian. In fact the country is already with field commanders.
- Autumn 200 — the **Battle of Guandu**. Cao Cao with a smaller army smashes Yuan Shao. A turning point: northern China begins to gather into one pair of hands. In 20 years Han will fall; the Three Kingdoms begin (Wei, Shu, Wu).
- In May Sun Ce is mortally wounded; power in the lower Yangtze passes to younger brother Sun Quan — future founder of the kingdom of Wu.
- The Yellow Turban rebellion was back in 184. Since then central power has never got back on its feet. Officials steal, generals cut each other, peasants flee.
- The Western Regions (Silk Road) Han barely holds anymore. Buddhism in China is already no rarity.

**Parthia**  
- After three Roman campaigns in a century (Trajan, Lucius Verus, Severus) the Arsacid realm wobbles. Courts quarrel, vassals act up.
- A quarter-century left to the end: in 224–226 the Persian Ardashir will overthrow the last Parthians and found the Sasanian Empire — a far tougher enemy for Rome.

**Kushans and India**  
- The Kushan Empire after Kanishka is no longer what it was. Around this time Vasudeva is mentioned; the state fragments.
- In Malwa, Rudrasena I of the Western Satraps (Sakas) is in power.
- Hinduism takes on the form we recognize later; Buddhism lives, but northern India's political map is patchy.

**Far East**  
- In Japan tradition links this time with Himiko of Yamatai — a shaman-ruler who gathers the clans. Dates are conditional, but the direction is right: from a scatter of villages a proto-state is being molded.
- Korea: Goguryeo, Baekje, Silla are already full kingdoms.

### The rest of the world, briefly
- In Mesoamerica around this time the Maya Classic period begins. Teotihuacan is already a huge city.
- In the Andes the Paracas culture fades; other societies rise (including Moche a little earlier / around this).
- Aksum in Africa strengthens and trades through the Red Sea.

### What it feels like
Year 200 — the moment when the old giants are still alive, but no longer young.

Rome has just beaten Parthia again and pretends everything is under control. China is formally still Han, but in truth already a war of all against all, and Cao Cao has just won the main round. Parthia is living out its days. The Kushans crumble. Along the roads silk, pepper, and glass still travel, but the states that guarded those roads begin to fall apart almost at once.

In 20 years there will be no Han. In 25 — no Parthia. Rome will last longer, but no longer as in Trajan's century: ahead lie soldier-emperors, crises, and new Persians in the east.

Year 200 — quiet outside, very loud inside.
