Year **800** is remembered for one scene: Christmas, the basilica of Saint Peter, Pope Leo III places a crown on the head of the king of the Franks. The West again calls someone emperor. Byzantium does not recognise it. Baghdad watches from the side and sends an elephant.

### Europe
- **25 December.** Charlemagne, already king of the Franks and the Lombards, rose from prayer — and became “Augustus,” emperor of the Romans. The crowd shouts: life and victory. The pope then kneels. Charlemagne, according to Einhard, was not delighted by the surprise: the crown came not from the people and not from Constantinople, but from the bishop of Rome. So was born the idea of a western empire, from which the Holy Roman Empire would later grow.
- Before that, a whole year of politics. In 799 Leo III barely kept his eyes and tongue, fled to Charlemagne at Paderborn. In autumn Charlemagne came to Rome, examined the pope’s case; on 23 December Leo swore a purgatory oath. That same day they brought from Jerusalem the keys to the Holy Sepulchre and the city’s banner — a gesture from the patriarch and the caliphate: Charlemagne as protector of the holy places.
- Charlemagne’s realm: the Gauls, Germany to the Elbe (the Saxons still being finished off), northern Italy, northern Spain (the Spanish March), influence in Rome. Aachen is being built as a “second Rome.” Schools, minuscule script, capitularies — the Carolingian Renaissance is already underway.
- Vikings are already probing the shores: Shetland, Orkney, watch on the Atlantic. Real raids are a matter of the coming decades.
- England is still a heptarchy. Asturias holds a tiny Christian north of Spain. Córdoba is an Umayyad emirate, enemy of both Charlemagne and the Abbasids.

### Byzantium
- On the throne **Irene**, the first woman ruling alone. For the pope this is a handy pretext: “the throne is empty, a woman does not count.” Hence Charlemagne’s coronation.
- The empire holds Anatolia, Greece in pieces, Sicily, southern Italy, the islands. Bulgaria is strong. With the Abbasids — now war, now tribute. Irene even thought of marrying Charlemagne — in Constantinople that thought will later make them shudder, and they will overthrow her (802).

### The Islamic world
- **Abbasid Caliphate**, capital Baghdad. Caliph **Harun al-Rashid** — the one from the tales of the Thousand and One Nights, but a living politician. The state from Ifriqiya to Khorasan, though the Maghrib already lives its own life, and Spain is separate, under the Umayyads.
- With Charlemagne — a strange friendship against common foes (Córdoba and sometimes Byzantium). Envoys travel, gifts fly. The elephant Abul-Abbas will arrive in 802.
- This is still the golden age of the caliphate: translation thought, trade, paper, cities.

### Asia
**China — Tang**  
- Emperor Dezong. After the An Lushan rebellion (755–763) the empire is no longer what it was: military governors stronger than the centre; on the borders Tibetans and Uyghurs. Chang’an is still huge, culture brilliant, but the peak of political power is behind.

**Japan** — Heian is only just beginning (in 794 the capital moved to Heian-kyō, today’s Kyoto). Chinese model + its own path.

**India** is fragmented. On the seas, Srivijaya.

### What it feels like
Year 800 — Europe’s fork in the road.

Three “Romes” look at each other: Constantinople (the old), Aachen/Rome (the new), and between them Baghdad, which does not pretend to be Rome but is richer than both. Charlemagne gathered almost the whole Latin West. The caliphate gathered almost the whole Near East and North Africa. China is unto itself.

The coronation did not resurrect ancient Rome. It fixed another order: a Germanic king + a Roman pope + the idea of empire without a real Constantinople. Byzantium is offended. The Muslim world is busy with its own affairs. Vikings are already saddling the sea.

In fourteen years Charlemagne will die; his grandsons will carve up the empire. In nine years Harun will die. The Tang will last another century, gasping. And the word “emperor of the West” will hang over Europe for more than a thousand years.
