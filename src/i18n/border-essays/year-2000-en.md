Year **2000**. Midnight is met with fireworks in every time zone. Computers were predicted an end of the world over two digits in the date — almost nothing broke. Something else broke: the internet bubble, the American election, peace in the Middle East. In Russia a new president. In Belgrade a bulldozer drives at parliament. Sixteen months remain until 11 September.

### The Year 2000 problem
Many programs stored the year as two digits: 99, then 00. People feared that banks, power stations, planes and missiles would decide that 1900 had arrived. Governments and companies spent years fixing code. On the night of 1 January the world held its breath.

There were small glitches. No catastrophe. Afterwards some said: “panic for nothing.” Others: “nothing happened because they fixed it.” Both are a little true.

That same New Year’s night Boris Yeltsin on television unexpectedly resigns. Acting president becomes **Vladimir Putin**. That is not a computer glitch. That is a change of era in Russia.

### Russia
On 26 March Putin is elected president in the first round. The country after the nineties wants order. The Second Chechen War is on: Grozny was already taken in winter, the war is not over.

On 12 August in the Barents Sea on exercises the nuclear submarine **Kursk** sinks. 118 people. For several days the country watches as the authorities say one thing and no one can be saved. The first big blow to the image of “everything under control.”

Only a few percent use the internet in Russia. Not everyone has a mobile yet. Oil is rising — the economy after the 1998 crisis begins to catch its breath.

### The United States
The president is still Bill Clinton, last year. On 7 November the election: Vice President **Al Gore** against Texas governor **George W. Bush**.

Gore wins more votes nationwide. The winner is whoever takes the Electoral College. Everything hinges on **Florida**: the margin is hundreds of ballots. Punch-card holes (“chads”), “butterflies” on the forms, recount, courts. On 12 December the US Supreme Court stops the recount. Bush becomes president.

This is not a coup. It is a lawful but very crooked victory. Trust in elections cracks. In a year the same Bush will be president on 11 September.

On 12 October in the port of Aden (Yemen) suicide bombers in a boat blow up the destroyer **USS Cole**. 17 sailors. Al-Qaeda is already hitting Americans. Most of the country still does not grasp the scale.

### The Middle East
In summer at Camp David Clinton brings together Israeli prime minister Barak and Yasser Arafat. No deal.

In September the **Second Intifada** begins. The pretext is Ariel Sharon’s visit to the Temple Mount. Then — stones, shooting, bombings, air strikes, years of blood. The peace process of the nineties effectively ends there.

### Europe and Yugoslavia
Slobodan Milošević loses the election and will not leave. On 5 October in Belgrade the crowd takes parliament and television. History will call it the “Bulldozer Revolution”: they actually drive construction machinery at the building. Milošević gives in. Yugoslavia after the wars of the nineties turns toward another power. Later Milošević will be handed to the tribunal in The Hague.

### Asia
In June the leaders of North and South Korea meet for the first time — Kim Jong-il and Kim Dae-jung. Hope of a thaw. It will not last long.

In Taiwan they elect Chen Shui-bian president — not from the old Kuomintang party. Beijing tenses.

China is not yet in the WTO (it will join in 2001), but already the workshop of the world in the making.

### Technology, money, sport
In spring the Nasdaq index, where the internet companies sit, hits a record — and begins to crumble. The **dot-com bubble**: firms without profit were worth billions, then the shares went to zero. The internet did not stop living. What burned were the fantasies that any site with “.com” was a gold mine.

In Japan PlayStation 2 comes out. Later it will become the best-selling console of its generation.

On 2 November the first permanent crew arrives at the International Space Station. People have lived in orbit without a break ever since.

In September the Olympics in Sydney. For Australia — a festival for the whole world.

On 25 July near Paris **Concorde** crashes. Supersonic passenger flights after that live only a little longer and die.

In September at the UN they adopt the Millennium Declaration. From it they will later make the Millennium Development Goals: poverty, schools, AIDS. Some will work, some will stay on paper.

### What it feels like
The year 2000 they wanted as a clean sheet. What they got was a draft.

The computers did not collapse. The myth that internet stocks only rise did. America for the first time in a long while cannot quickly say who its president is. Russia changes the head of state without a tank in the street — and at once gets the *Kursk*. Israelis and Palestinians are at war again. Serbia removes the man because of whom half a decade Yugoslavia burned.

The world is still unipolar: the USA the only superpower. China rises quietly. Phones mostly call, they do not shoot video. News is waited for in the evening on television.

What follows:
- 2001 — Wikipedia; 11 September; war in Afghanistan;
- 2003 — Iraq;
- 2004 — EU enlargement; Beslan;
- 2008 — the world financial crisis.

2000 is the year of fireworks and a hanging chad on the ballot. They began celebrating the millennium. For many the real twenty-first century will begin on 11 September 2001.
