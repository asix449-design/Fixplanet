Year **1100**. Jerusalem has been in crusader hands for two years. The man they would not call king — Godfrey of Bouillon — dies in summer. At Christmas his brother Baldwin puts on the crown in Bethlehem. In England a king is killed by an arrow on the hunt. In Rus the princes, after a fierce quarrel, swear again to live in peace. In China an emperor who loves painting more than war takes the throne. To Genghis Khan — another hundred and six years.

### The Holy Land
The First Crusade has already happened. In 1099 the knights took Jerusalem — cruelly, with a massacre. Godfrey of Bouillon refused to be “king where Christ wore a crown of thorns,” and took the title Defender of the Holy Sepulchre. On 18 July 1100 he dies. They argue: typhus, a wound, poison. No matter. The throne must be filled.

His brother **Baldwin**, until then count of Edessa, goes south. Patriarch Daimbert, Pisa’s man, wants the holy city to be ecclesiastical. The knights want a king. On 25 December in the Church of the Nativity in Bethlehem Baldwin is crowned. The **Kingdom of Jerusalem** appears. Nearby — Antioch, Edessa, soon Tripoli. A narrow ribbon of fortresses on a foreign shore.

Bohemond, prince of Antioch, that same year falls captive to the Danishmends. Tancred becomes regent. Haifa is taken with help from the Venetian fleet. Without Italian ships these states would have choked at once.

The Muslim world is not one. Seljuks fight each other. The Egyptian Fatimids want Palestine back. That is how the crusaders live: while the enemies have not yet fused into one Saladin — and to him almost a century.

Pope Paschal II calls new people to the East. The “crusade of 1101” will go — later they will call it the crusade of those who funked the first time. Seljuks in Anatolia will smash them in pieces.

### Europe
England. On 2 August in the New Forest on a hunt an arrow hits king **William Rufus**, son of the Conqueror. Officially — an accident. The shooter was supposedly the vassal Tirel; he fled at once. Younger brother **Henry I** does not wait for the funeral: gallops to Winchester for the treasury and in three days is crowned at Westminster. Elder brother Robert is still on crusade. So the barons will not revolt, Henry issues a “Charter of Liberties” — promises not to rob them as Rufus did. This is not the Great Charter of 1215, but the paper already exists: the king limits himself in writing.

France. King Philip I — a scandalous man; for his marriage they even excommunicated him. Real power gradually passes to his son Louis, the future Fat. The Capetians are not yet France from sea to sea, but Paris is no longer a village.

The Empire. Henry IV still quarrels with popes — an echo of the Investiture Contest. The Hohenstaufen are ahead.

Spain. The Reconquista goes on. El Cid died in 1099. The Almoravids from Africa hold the south and press Valencia. The Christian kingdoms in the north are not united, but they no longer retreat for good.

The Normans recently (1066) took England and have long sat in southern Italy and Sicily. Europe is tied by these people tighter than it seems.

### Rus
Kiev is still “mother of cities,” but the throne no longer means what it did under Yaroslav. Grand Prince — **Sviatopolk II Iziaslavich**. Beside him stronger and wiser — **Vladimir Monomakh**.

In 1097 at Liubech the princes agreed: each holds his patrimony, do not trespass on your brother. They broke it at once: they blinded Vasylko of Terebovlia. War followed. In August 1100 a congress at Uvetichi. Peace. Davyd Igorevich, the main troublemaker, is judged and moved to lesser towns. On paper unity against the Cumans again. In fact the appanages harden. “Feudal” Rus is exactly these congresses: they swore, rode apart, in a year a knife in the back.

Cumans now marry daughters to princes, now burn the fringes. The Pechenegs have already been beaten. The steppe has not gone away.

Novgorod on its own. Polotsk on its own. To the Mongols — far. To Moscow as capital — farther still.

### Asia
**China, Song dynasty.** In February Zhezong dies. His seventeen-year-old brother **Huizong** succeeds him — calligrapher, collector, aesthete. Kaifeng — one of the world’s largest cities, over a million with the garrison. The empire’s population about a hundred million. Gunpowder, compass, paper, printing already exist. Northern Khitans (Liao) sit at the border. The Jurchens are not yet the Jin empire. To the catastrophe of 1127, when the north will be lost — a quarter century. For now Song seems eternal and refined.

**Seljuks.** The great empire after Malik-Shah has sprawled into pieces: Iran, Syria, Rum in Anatolia. The Baghdad caliph under them like an honourable hostage.

**Byzantium.** Alexios I Komnenos — a rare emperor who knows how to wriggle free. He let the crusaders through his lands, recovered a piece of Asia Minor, did not let them sit on his neck. Constantinople is still rich and intact. The Latins will take it in a hundred and four years.

**Japan.** End of the Heian age. Samurai already matter more than court poets. To the first shogunate — ninety years.

### Africa and beyond
Fatimids in Egypt — Shia, rivals of the Baghdad caliphs and the Seljuks. It was they who lost Jerusalem on the eve of the crusaders’ arrival.

West Africa piles up gold. America does not show its kingdoms to Europe. Vikings in America are already a forgotten episode.

### What it feels like
Year 1100 — the morning after a miracle that turned out to be a massacre and a state.

Europe for the first time in a long while has planted its fortress in the heart of someone else’s holy place. It thinks God is clearly for it: otherwise how could a handful of people from the edge of the world take Jerusalem? The Near East thinks otherwise: the Franks have come; we will wait; they will tear each other apart.

In England power goes to whoever galloped fastest to the treasury. In Rus power is shared by those who know how to say “peace” and not keep it. In China power is taken by brushes and silk — until from the north come people without calligraphy.

What follows:
- 1101 — smash of the “second” crusader wave in Anatolia;
- 1111 — Henry V and the pope, end of this phase of the Investiture Contest;
- 1113 — death of Sviatopolk, Kiev to Monomakh;
- 1187 — Saladin will take Jerusalem back;
- 1204 — crusaders will smash Constantinople;
- 1206 — Genghis Khan.

1100 is the year of a crown in Bethlehem and an arrow in an English forest. The holy and the accidental happen in one calendar. That is how the Middle Ages work.
