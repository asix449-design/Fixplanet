The **2nd century** — roughly the years from 101 to 200. For Rome it looks like a "golden age" and the start of illness inside. For China — a century when great Han still stands at first, and by the end is already cracking. Christ was crucified in the previous century. Christians are still few; sometimes tolerated, sometimes executed; there is no great church empire. Nobody yet reckons an era "from the Nativity."

If the 1st century was the age of Augustus and the destruction of the Jerusalem Temple, the 2nd is the age of emperors later called "good," and of a plague that will show the world is not elastic.

### Rome
After Domitian the Senate exhaled. On the throne a line of men the textbook will later arrange in a row: Trajan, Hadrian, Antoninus Pius, Marcus Aurelius. Then — Commodus. With Commodus the fairy tale ends.

**Trajan** (98–117) — the last who seriously pushes the borders forward. Takes Dacia (today's Romania: gold, colonists, a bridge over the Danube). Then drives at Parthia, reaches the Persian Gulf. The empire at its maximum. The map is beautiful. Holding it is already hard.

**Hadrian** (117–138) understands this better. Gives back part of the eastern conquests, travels the provinces, builds a wall in Britain. Not "forward," but "close the perimeter." Soldier and official matter more to him than triumph.

**Antoninus Pius** (138–161) — quiet: few wars, many courts and grain. Later Romans will remember this as the norm. It was not the norm. It was a pause.

**Marcus Aurelius** (161–180) — an emperor with a notebook. Writes *Meditations* on campaign. On the Danube frontier Germans and Sarmatians push in waves. In the east, Parthia again. In the 165–180s the **Antonine Plague** walks the empire — most likely smallpox. Armies melt, cities empty, prices jump. The philosopher on the throne fights to death at Vindobona (Vienna).

His son **Commodus** (180–192) wants to be Hercules in the circus, not Marcus in the saddle. They kill him. Then a short nightmare: a year of several emperors, then Septimius Severus — a soldier from Africa. By 200 Rome is no longer "Senate and citizens." Rome is an army that chooses its master.

Law, roads, baths, amphitheaters — all of it works. In the provinces they speak Latin and Greek. A man from Spain can become emperor. A man from Syria — a senator. That is the Roman focus of the 2nd century: the empire is wider than Italy. Italy is no longer the only center, only the vainest.

Christians are sometimes persecuted (under Marcus Aurelius there were executions), sometimes ignored. For most people this is a strange sect without sacrifices and without statues. Judaism after the smash of 70 and the Bar Kokhba revolt (132–135) has lost the Temple and Jerusalem as a Jewish city: Hadrian rebuilds it as Aelia Capitolina. Diaspora becomes the main form of life.

### Parthia and the east
Parthia is still the main neighbor. Wars with Trajan and with Lucius Verus under Marcus Aurelius are hard on both sides. The Parthian court itself is in feuds. By the end of the century a new Persian force will begin to sprout from this mess — the Sasanians. They will take the stage in the 3rd century and hit harder than the Parthians.

The Kushan Empire at the junction of India, Central Asia, and the roads to China — the 2nd century is exactly its strength. Kanishka and his heirs. Buddhism rides these roads east. Gold of Roman coins turns up in Indian hoards: pepper, textiles, ivory go west.

### China
The dynasty is **Eastern Han**. Court in Luoyang. On paper — continuation of the ancient empire. In practice eunuchs, empresses' clans, generals.

Until 184 you can still pretend the center holds the Middle Kingdom. In 184 — the **Yellow Turban** rebellion. Religion, hunger, "Heaven has withdrawn from Han." The empire will not be eaten formally at once, but the spine is broken. By 200 the country is already in the hands of field commanders. Soon the "Three Kingdoms": Wei, Shu, Wu. The novel about it will be written a thousand years later. The blood is now.

To the north, the steppe. The Xiongnu are no longer the force they were under Western Han, but the border is alive. Silk still goes west. Paper and ink — Chinese everyday life, not a marvel.

### India and beyond
Northern India after the Kushans and local dynasties. The south trades with Rome through the Red Sea: the monsoon is the timetable. In Southeast Asia Indian stories and Buddhism settle onto local kingdoms.

Japan is still pre-state by Han standards: rice, clans, burial mounds. Korea — early kingdoms, a strong Chinese cultural wind.

### North of Rome and the "barbarians"
Beyond the Rhine and Danube the German world is denser and more organized than under Augustus. Marcomanni, Quadi; the Goths are not yet the Goths who will take Rome in two centuries, but the pressure is already constant. Rome answers with walls, rivers, subsidies, and war.

On the steppes — Sarmatians. On the lands of future Rus' — forest cultures, no state. Slavic principalities are far off.

Roman Africa — a granary and cities along the shore. Farther south — their own kingdoms; Rome knows them poorly. America is still another humanity: Teotihuacan in Mexico is just rising, a giant city without a single "emperor" in the Roman sense.

### Plague and numbers
The Antonine Plague is the main invisible character of the century's second half. Death tolls are argued, but armies and Mediterranean cities feel the blow. After this Rome finds it harder to raise legions "as before." The 3rd century will inherit a shortage of people and money.

On earth there are still a couple of hundred million people. China and India hold the lion's share. The Roman Empire — tens of millions, huge for the West, not the center of the planet's population.

### What it feels like
The 2nd century — a century of good plaster over old cracks.

A Roman in Ephesus goes to the bath, argues in court, looks at the emperor's statue and thinks it will always be so. A soldier on the Danube knows that "always" is the next barbarian crossing. An official in Luoyang writes a report to the emperor and does not know which eunuch will burn him tomorrow. A merchant in Alexandria loads pepper and does not think of Jesus. A Christian in the catacombs (catacombs as an image a little later, but the meaning is that) thinks of the end of times and of how not to get caught.

The world is better connected than in year 1: ship, caravan, coin, letter. But connected fragilely. One epidemic, one bad heir, one "yellow" revolt — and the golden age turns out to be an intermission.

What follows, already in the 3rd century:
- 212 — citizenship for almost all free people of the empire (Caracalla);
- 224–226 — Sasanians instead of Parthia;
- 235–284 — the 3rd-century crisis in Rome: emperors for a year, breakaway Gaul and Palmyra;
- 220 — official end of Han;
- 284 — Diocletian will begin to glue Rome back together, already as a different machine.

The 2nd century is remembered for Trajan's Column, Hadrian's Wall, and Marcus Aurelius's little book. Worth remembering also the plague, and Commodus, and the Yellow Turbans. The plaster was excellent. The wall under it — not.
