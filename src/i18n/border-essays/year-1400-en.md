Year **1400**. Europe is still medieval to the bone: the plague has not long passed, the Hundred Years' War is not over, the church has two popes at once. And the main man of the year does not live in Paris or Rome. His name is **Timur**, in Europe — Tamerlane. The lame man from Samarkand who in thirty years built an empire from Delhi to Damascus and is now driving into Syria.

Columbus is not even a project yet. A hundred years until Cabral's Brazil. The world is not yet "discovered." It is already big — Europe simply knows a piece of it.

### Europe
England. King Richard II was deposed a year ago and, most likely, starved to death in a castle. On the throne sits his cousin — Henry IV, the first Lancaster. The country is boiling. In Wales Owain Glyndŵr rises: a long revolt, his own almost-country, war with the English for years ahead.

France in the Hundred Years' War. The English still hold pieces. Fifteen years until Agincourt (1415). King Charles VI is at times not himself — literally: fits of madness. The dukes of Orléans and Burgundy already hate each other. Soon this will become a civil war inside the French war.

The church is split. They call it the Great Schism: one pope in Rome, another in Avignon. Both curse each other. For an ordinary person it looks like this: unclear whose mass is the right one, and an indulgence can be bought from both.

The Holy Roman Empire — a set of princes. Bohemia on the eve of the Hussite wars, but Jan Hus still preaches, does not burn.

In the north the Hanse trades herring and cloth. In the east Poland and Lithuania are already linked by personal union: one man, Jogaila, is king of Poland, and his cousin Vytautas is grand duke of Lithuania. Together in ten years they will smash the Teutonic Order at Grunwald. In 1400 that is still being prepared.

Byzantium is living out its days. Constantinople — a capital without an empire, the Ottomans already around it. In 1400 the Turks could have finished the city earlier, but Timur climbs onto their necks. That will accidentally gift Constantinople another half-century.

### Timur and the Near East
Timur is not Chinggis Khan and not "just a Mongol." He is a Turk from Mawarannahr, lame, literate in his own way, cruel as a force of nature. The capital is Samarkand, which he rebuilds with pyramids of skulls and blue domes.

In 1398 he already sacked Delhi. In 1395 he crushed Tokhtamysh, khan of the Golden Horde, and burned Yelets on the edge of the Russian lands. In 1400 it is Syria's turn, then under the Mamluks of Egypt.

He takes Sivas, Aleppo. People are killed by the tens of thousands; craftsmen are driven off to Samarkand. In 1401 there will be Damascus and slaughter again. In 1402 at Ankara Timur will smash the Ottoman sultan Bayezid the Thunderbolt and take him prisoner. The Ottoman Empire will fall apart for ten years into a war of sons. Europe will later say "thank you to the barbarian": without that victory the Turks might have taken Constantinople not in 1453.

Timur himself will die in 1405 at Otrar on the road to China. The empire will almost immediately crumble. But in 1400 he seems a man to whom the map is no law.

### The Russian lands
Moscow. Grand Prince **Vasily I**, son of Dmitry Donskoy. Kulikovo Field is already twenty years past, but the Horde has not vanished — it is simply sick.

The Golden Horde after Timur's smashings is in disarray. Khans change; often the temnik Edigu holds power. Moscow can breathe a little easier, but the yarlyk and the tribute have gone nowhere. In 1408 Edigu will still come and burn everything around Moscow.

The main neighbour to the west is not the Horde, but Vytautas's Lithuania. Vytautas has already almost swallowed Smolensk (the finish — in 1404). Vasily is married to Vytautas's daughter, and that does not stop them a few years later from standing armies against each other on the Ugra — and parting without a great battle.

Novgorod is still its own, Tver is still alive, Ryazan is still separate. There is no "Russia" in our sense. There is Moscow, already the strongest of the Russian seats, but not the only one.

### Asia further on
**China.** The Ming dynasty replaced the Mongol Yuan only a little over thirty years ago. Now civil war: the uncle, Prince of Yan Zhu Di, fights the nephew-emperor. In 1400 at the Baihe River Zhu Di smashes the imperial army. In two years he will take Nanjing, become the Yongle Emperor, move the capital to Beijing and start building the Forbidden City. His admiral Zheng He will later lead a huge fleet into the Indian Ocean. In 1400 that is not yet a fleet, but a civil war.

**The Ottomans** at their peak before Timur: the Balkans under them, Constantinople in a ring. After Ankara 1402 — a pause.

**India.** Delhi after Timur is in ruins. The Delhi Sultanate has not died, but its spine is broken.

At Malacca around this time Parameswara founds a port that will become the main spice crossroads of Southeast Asia. In a hundred years the Portuguese will come here.

**Japan** in its fragmentation. The Ashikaga shoguns weaken; the princes are stronger than the centre.

### Africa and the Americas
In Egypt the Mamluks — a military caste that has ruled for a century and a half. They are just getting their faces smashed by Timur in Syria.

In West Africa the great Sahel kingdoms are still alive; gold goes across the Sahara to the Mediterranean. The Portuguese will take Ceuta only in 1415. There is no ocean route around Africa.

In the Americas their own empires are forming or in strength: the Aztecs already collect tribute in Mexico; the Incas in the Andes are only beginning their great push. More than a century until the Europeans. For them 1400 is simply their own year.

### What it feels like
Year 1400 — the year when the most terrifying man on earth rides from Samarkand to Aleppo, and in London a new king is not sure he will sit until winter.

The plague of the fourteenth century no longer mows down half a street at a time, but the world after it is thin: fewer people, lands empty, prices and manners shifted. Knights still in armour, but gunpowder is no longer a toy. There is no printing. News travels for months.

Europe is busy with itself: two popes, two dynasties, one endless war. Asia is busy with Timur and dynastic change. Africa and the Americas live by their own calendars.

### What follows
- 1402 — the Ankara catastrophe of the Ottomans;
- 1405 — Timur's death, Zheng He's first voyage;
- 1410 — Grunwald;
- 1415 — Agincourt and Portuguese Ceuta;
- 1453 — the fall of Constantinople.

1400 — the last great year when a man from the steppe could with one campaign put on pause the Turks, and the Egyptian sultans, and someone else's China in his plans. After him an empire of that scale could no longer be glued together from one saddle. What remained would be states with a chancery, a cannon and a fleet. Timur will not live to see that. In 1400 he is still in the saddle.
