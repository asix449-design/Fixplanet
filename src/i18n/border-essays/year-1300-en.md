Year **1300**. The pope declares a jubilee: come to Rome — we will remit sins. So many people that on the bridge they set up one-way traffic. On the surface the church is at the summit. In fact the king of France is already preparing a slap for it. The Mongol Empire is still huge, but no longer one whole. In a corner of Asia Minor a small bey named Osman bites the Byzantine edge. From that scrap will later grow an empire that will eat Constantinople.

There is no plague yet. No printing. No America for Europe. The age of crusades has almost run out of breath: the Holy Land was lost already in 1291.

### Europe
France under **Philip IV the Fair**. The king is handsome, the state is hard. He needs money for war with England and with Flanders — rich cloth towns that do not want to be merely a French cashbox. He taxes even the church. The pope rages.

England. King Edward I, “Hammer of the Scots.” William Wallace has already been executed (1298). Scotland has not surrendered; Robert Bruce is still ahead. In 1300 the Swedes plant the fortress Landskrona at the mouth of the Neva — opposite the future Petersburg. The Novgorodians will later smoke them out.

Italy is sliced into city-states. The Florentine florin is already counted in foreign lands — a rarity for money then. Dante has not finished the *Comedy* yet, but already lives in this politics of knives and exile. Sicily after the “Vespers” of 1282 is still an apple of discord between Anjou and Aragon.

Pope **Boniface VIII** stages the first great jubilee year. Rome earns on pilgrims. The pope himself appears now in priest’s vestments, now almost as emperor: spiritual power, he says, is above royal. In three years Philip the Fair’s men will seize him at Anagni. Boniface will not survive the humiliation. Then the popes will long be dragged to Avignon. 1300 is a parade portrait of a power that has already cracked.

Germany — Holy Roman Empire without a firm hand. The Habsburgs are not yet what they will become.

On the Pyrenees their own kingdoms: Castile, Aragon, Portugal, and in the south still the Emirate of Granada — the last scrap of Muslim Spain. Bilbao is founded around this year. To Columbus almost two centuries.

### Byzantium and the Turks
Constantinople the Greeks recovered already in 1261, driving out the Latin crusaders. The empire is a stump: the capital, scraps of Greece, western Asia Minor. No money. They hire whoever turns up.

Around 1300 in Bithynia, not far from Nicaea and Nicomedia, rises the beylik of **Osman**. This is one of many Turkish appanages after the collapse of the Sultanate of Rum. Osman beats Byzantine detachments, settles on villages, does not yet storm the capital. In two years will be the battle of Bapheus. In a century and a half his descendants will take Constantinople. In 1300 it looks like a border gang, not like world history.

### The Mongols
Genghis Khan’s empire is formally still from Korea to the steppes by Rus, but it is already four big pieces:

- in China — the **Yuan** dynasty, heirs of Kublai. Capital Dadu, the future Beijing. Chinese live under them, sit exams, seethe. To the expulsion of the Mongols and the Ming — 68 years;
- in the steppe and over Rus — the **Golden Horde**;
- in Iran — the Hulaguids, already weakening;
- in the Chagatai ulus — its own mess.

Mongols still go on campaigns: Syria, Burma. But this is no longer “the whole world at one kurultai.” These are shards with one name.

In Rus princes ride to the Horde for yarlyks. In 1300 or 1301 they gather at Dmitrov — a rare congress, against the background that in the Horde dual power has just collapsed: Nogai, the mighty temnik, has died. Grand Prince of Vladimir in these years — Andrei Alexandrovich, son of Alexander Nevsky, not the most beloved character among descendants. Moscow is not yet first. Tver is stronger. Kalita is ahead.

Novgorod trades with the Hansa. Kiev has long not been the capital.

### Asia further
**India.** The Delhi Sultanate. Muslim sultans hold the north; the south is still its own. Mongols were beaten back from Delhi quite recently.

**Japan.** Kamakura shogunate. Japan beat off the Mongol fleets of 1274 and 1281 — helped by the “divine wind,” the typhoon. To a new turmoil not far.

Southeast Asia: on Java Majapahit rises. The Mongol landing there in the 1290s ended in disgrace.

### Africa and beyond
In West Africa the Mali Empire gathers strength. In twelve years Mansa Musa will go on hajj and shower Cairo with gold so that prices there will be knocked sideways for years.

The Mamluks in Egypt — the only ones who in the thirteenth century stopped the Mongols in open field (Ain Jalut, 1260). They also drove the crusaders out of Acre.

America lives by its own kingdoms. The Aztecs are not yet those, the Incas not yet those. Europe has no one to tell it about this.

### What it feels like
Year 1300 — a jubilee on a crack.

The pope sells forgiveness in Rome and quarrels with a king who has a better army and a worse conscience. Florence mints a coin that foreign merchants trust. Osman herds sheep and cuts border fortresses. The Horde still collects tribute from Russian princes, but can no longer strike to one end of Eurasia as under Batu.

People live densely: Europe after two calm centuries is cramped again; forests are cut, cities grow. That is exactly why in half a century the plague will cut so many — there is someone to cut.

What follows:
- 1302 — the “Matins of Bruges” and the Battle of the Spurs: Flemish townsmen beat French knights;
- 1303 — a slap to the pope at Anagni;
- 1307–1314 — the destruction of the Templars;
- 1337 — start of the Hundred Years’ War;
- 1347 — the Black Death in Europe;
- 1368 — Ming drives the Mongols from China;
- 1453 — Ottomans take Constantinople.

1300 is the year when the Middle Ages still stand in full vestments and you can already hear the seams crack. A pilgrim in Rome thinks he has defended his soul. A king in Paris thinks he has defended the treasury. Osman thinks about the next valley. All three are right for their short term.
