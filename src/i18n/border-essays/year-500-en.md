Year **500**. The Western Roman Empire as an office has been gone a quarter-century: in 476 the last emperor in Italy was removed. The city of Rome itself stands. The Senate still sits. But the master of the peninsula is the Ostrogothic king **Theodoric**. He rules from Ravenna, wears a Roman cloak and Gothic power at once.

In the East the empire lives. In Constantinople Justinian will soon sit — for now he is not yet emperor. China is still cut into north and south. In India the Gupta realm is no longer what it was. On the steppe — their own khaganates. Britain after the Romans' departure sinks into wars of petty kings.

This is no longer "late Rome." This is the start of the world later called the early Middle Ages. Only people then do not call their age that.

### Italy and the West
Theodoric took Italy in 493. Odoacer, the very man who overthrew the last emperor, he eliminated. Then a rare construction for the century: Goths — army and top, Romans — Senate, taxes, laws. Officially Theodoric as if rules "in the name of" the eastern emperor. In fact he is king at home.

Ravenna is rich in mosaics. Rome lives on memory. Roads are still Roman. Schools and baths fewer. Latin remains the language of law.

Gaul is no longer a Roman province. There the kingdom of the **Franks**. Clovis has just, in 496 or around then, been baptized. What matters is not only that he became a Christian. What matters is which kind: in the Nicene pattern, as in Constantinople and among the Roman population, not Arian like Goths and Vandals. Because of that a Frankish king finds it easier to deal with Gallo-Roman bishops. In two or three centuries this will grow into the core of future France.

Visigoths sit in Spain and still a piece of southern Gaul. Vandals — in Africa, capital Carthage. They cut the old Roman link "Africa feeds Italy." The sea between them and Italy became dangerous.

Burgundians — in the Rhône valley. In Britain Roman legions have long been gone. On the island Celtic rulers and Angles, Saxons, Jutes come from the continent. Of King Arthur a legend will later be made. In 500 on the ground — mud, forts, and petty battles, not a round table.

### Eastern empire
Constantinople is the real capital of the Mediterranean. Emperor Anastasius: thrifty, clever, fills the treasury. Exactly this money Justinian will later spend on wars and temples.

Borders on the Danube restless. In the east — Persia. Egypt and Syria still theirs: grain, tax, priests' disputes.

The church already argues not whether to be Christian, but how exactly to speak of Christ's nature. From these disputes comes politics. Egypt and Syria often disagree with the capital. The schism is not yet named "forever," but the crack is there.

Justinian in 500 is still a nephew at court, not the master. In twenty-seven years he will try to take Italy and Africa back. It will work for a time. Italy will suffer from this "reconquista" hardly less than from the Goths.

### Persia
The Sasanians are strong. Shah's court, Zoroastrian priests, heavy cavalry. With Constantinople — eternal neighbor conflict: now peace, now war over border fortresses and Armenia. In 500 there is no decisive winner. Both states still look eternal.

### China
The south — southern dynasties, capital at Jiankang. Thin court, strong poetry, unreliable army.

The north by this time is gathered by **Northern Wei** — a dynasty of Xianbei stock. They are already heavily Sinicized: court language, dress, officials. There is even forced Sinicization of their own. The north again becomes a big state machine. Almost a century until China's new unification under Sui.

Buddhism in north and south is already their own, not an overseas curiosity. Cave temples, monks, translations.

### India and farther east
The Guptas weaken. From the west and the steppe the Hephthalites press — "White Huns." Great cities and temples have not gone anywhere; a single calm empire is already gone.

In Southeast Asia Indian influence: temples, writing, kingdoms. Japan shapes the Yamato court. Korea — three states, between China and each other.

### Steppe, Slavs, north
On the steppe after the Huns — new alliances. Attila gone since 453. His realm fell apart. People whom Roman authors begin more confidently to call Slavs are already closer to the Danube. These are not yet states. These are tribes, raids, settlements.

On the future Russian lands — forest and hillforts. No Khazars, no Kiev. No written "Russian history."

### Africa
Vandal Carthage — a kingdom of Arian Germans over a Romano-African population. Copts in Egypt live under Constantinople and argue with it about the faith. Aksum is still strong on the Red Sea.

West Africa has not yet gathered its great empires on the Mali scale. The Sahara works by caravans.

### What it feels like
Year 500 — a year of kings on the rubble of one map.

Theodoric in Ravenna wants to be Goth and Roman at once. Clovis in France wants to be a king the bishops will accept. The emperor in Constantinople counts Italy his by right and for now does not go to take it. A Chinese official in the north teaches children already in Chinese writing, though the grandfathers spoke otherwise. A Persian shah looks at the Euphrates, not the Rhine.

The Roman world has not vanished. It has broken into languages and courts. Latin in the West is still law. Greek in the East is already the soul of the state. Barbarian kings wear Roman rings and mint coin with an imperial profile — sometimes not even their own.

What follows:
- 507 — Clovis beats the Visigoths at Vouillé; France shifts south;
- 527 — Justinian emperor;
- 533–534 — his general Belisarius takes Africa from the Vandals;
- 535–554 — Gothic War in Italy; the peninsula burns out;
- 541 — Justinianic Plague;
- 552 — Japan officially accepts Buddhism;
- 581–589 — Sui glues China together again.

Year 500 — a quiet-looking year between the fall of the West and the East's attempt to take everything back. In Ravenna you can still believe Rome continues by other hands. In Constantinople they already count how much it will cost. The price will be higher than it seems.
