Year **1800**. Napoleon is not yet emperor — he is First Consul, the man who has already taken power in France after the Revolution. In June he nearly loses a battle in Italy and wins it the same day — and after that he is almost impossible to remove. In America they haul the capital to a half-built Washington. In Russia Paul I quarrels with yesterday’s allies and flirts with Bonaparte. A little over a year remains until Paul’s murder.

### Europe and Napoleon
France after the coup of 18 Brumaire (November 1799) is no longer that revolutionary republic of endless assemblies. Napoleon Bonaparte, a general from Corsica, stands at the head of the state with two other consuls — but he decides.

The **War of the Second Coalition** is on: Austria and Britain against France (Russia is just leaving that company). In spring Napoleon leads an army over the Alps, through the Great St Bernard Pass — they drag the guns on sledges; it will become legend later.

**14 June — the Battle of Marengo** (northern Italy, near Alessandria). In the morning the Austrians nearly smash the French. By evening General Desaix’s reinforcement arrives. Desaix is killed, but the attack turns the battle. Napoleon goes from nearly beaten to “saviour of France.” Politically that matters more than the guns: after Marengo his power at home hardens. Austria begins to think of peace. They will sign it at Lunéville in February 1801.

The same day in Cairo they kill General Kléber — the man Napoleon left in command of the Egyptian army. The French still hold on in Egypt, but the expedition is already doomed: in 1801 they will have to leave.

On 2 April in Vienna they perform **Beethoven’s First Symphony** for the first time. Wars thunder nearby — and the music of the nineteenth century is already being written.

Napoleon founds the Bank of France. The state needs money and order in the finances after ten years of revolution.

In autumn the French hand Malta to the British. The island Napoleon took on the way to Egypt becomes British — and will stay so for a century and a half.

They elect a new pope: Pius VII. The previous one, Pius VI, the French had carted about as a prisoner; they bury him late.

### Russia
The emperor is **Paul I**, son of Catherine the Great. A sharp man, he loves order and drill, and does not like allies who behave like masters.

Recently the Russians fought the French together with the Turks and Austrians: Ushakov took Corfu, Suvorov marched through Italy and Switzerland. Suvorov dies in 1800. Paul takes offence at Austria and especially at England (the English will not give up Malta, though Paul considered himself patron of the Knights of Malta).

He reverses policy:
- seeks peace with Napoleon;
- gathers with Denmark, Sweden and Prussia an “armed neutrality” — in essence a league against British control of the seas;
- early in 1801 he will even send Cossacks on a mad march “to India” to frighten the English. They will not reach India. Paul will be killed in his own bedroom in March 1801. Officially they will say: a stroke. In fact — a guards’ conspiracy. His son Alexander I will take the throne.

In 1800 Georgia (Kartli-Kakheti) is already being prepared for annexation; the decree will come in January 1801, still under Paul. From there the wars with Persia and Turkey in the Caucasus will follow.

### Britain and Ireland
Parliament passes the **Act of Union**: from 1 January 1801 Great Britain and Ireland will become one kingdom — the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland. One monarch, one parliament in London. The Irish parliament is closed. Catholics still have almost no political rights. For Dublin it is a blow. For London — a way to hold the island after the rising of 1798.

Britain is at war with France almost without a break. The fleet is her main trump. Nelson is not yet Trafalgar (that is 1805), but the sea is already British.

The Dutch East India Company is legally dead from 1 January: the state takes the colonies for itself. The age of merchant company-empires is living out its last decades.

### The United States
The country is 24 years old. The president is John Adams, a Federalist. They move the capital from Philadelphia to **Washington**. On 1 November Adams is the first president to sleep in the White House — the house is still damp, unfinished.

In April they found the Library of Congress.

The presidential race of 1800 is on: Adams against Thomas Jefferson. The election will prove so dirty and tangled that the House of Representatives will choose the president. Jefferson will win. A peaceful change of parties — for a young republic not something that goes without saying.

Population about 5.3 million. Almost a million are slaves. In Philadelphia free Black people petition Congress against the slave trade.

With France they end the undeclared naval war (the Quasi-War): in September the treaty at Mortefontaine.

In three years Jefferson will buy Louisiana from Napoleon and double the territory of the United States. In 1800 they do not know that yet: Spain is just secretly returning Louisiana to France.

### Asia and the rest of the world
**China.** The Qing dynasty. The great emperor Qianlong abdicated in 1796 so as not to reign longer than his grandfather, but until 1799 he still stood behind his son. The country is huge, self-assured. In the 1790s Beijing politely showed the British Macartney embassy the door: “we have everything.” The Opium Wars are forty years away.

**India.** The British East India Company is no longer merely trading: it fights the Marathas and holds chunks of the subcontinent. The Mughal padishah in Delhi is more decoration than ruler.

The **Ottoman Empire** tries to drive the French out of Egypt. It goes badly until the English join in.

**Japan** is still closed. The Tokugawa shogunate.

In Haiti their own slave revolution is underway. France still hopes to reclaim the colony. In 1804 Haiti will become the first independent Black state.

### What it feels like
The year 1800 — a pause between revolution and empire.

The revolution in France is no longer the Jacobin meat-grinder and not yet an emperor’s court. Napoleon in a consul’s grey coat wins Italy and at the same time founds a bank. Beethoven writes his first symphony. Adams moves into the house they will later call the White House. Paul writes letters to Napoleon and fumes at London.

The world is still estate-bound, still sail-powered, still slave. But there are already the United States with a change of power through elections, France with a single civil code (they will adopt it in 1804), Britain with factories and coal.

What follows:
- 1801 — Union of Britain and Ireland; murder of Paul; Peace of Lunéville;
- 1804 — Napoleon emperor; independence of Haiti; the Napoleonic Code;
- 1805 — Trafalgar and Austerlitz;
- 1812 — the march on Russia;
- 1815 — Waterloo.

1800 is the year when the nineteenth century has already begun by the calendar and by feeling has not. You can still imagine that after Marengo a long peace will come. It will not.
